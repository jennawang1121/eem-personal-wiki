# Reading Guide

This personal wiki is based on four EEM revision documents. Start at [[index]], choose a topic, follow its related links, and use Sources to return to an unchanged Word document or its reading copy.

## Using the assistant

- **chat** helps plan revision, discuss ideas and shorten a previous answer. `/notes` explicitly requests evidence retrieval.
- **ask** answers an independent factual question using retrieved original text, with citations or an insufficient-evidence response.
- **search** displays original passages without calling Gemma.
- A small explicit bilingual keyword dictionary supports selected Chinese queries. It is not full semantic or multilingual retrieval.

## Originals and generated notes

The four unchanged Word documents are in `raw/`. Files in `sources/` contain verbatim extracted text and original images, not model summaries. Files in `wiki/` are Gemma drafts reviewed and corrected against the sources. Only original-source text enters retrieval; summaries, conversations and test answers do not become evidence.

Paragraph numbers follow Word's internal document order, including table cells and empty paragraphs. They are **not page numbers**. The reading copies show matching numbers for checking citations. All 22 embedded images are preserved for human viewing; no OCR or visual reasoning is used, so Gemma cannot see values found only inside figures.

All authored navigation and documentation is in English. Original bilingual source quotations and actual test responses remain unchanged to preserve evidence.

## Differences to keep in mind

- [[Energy Auctions]]: Quiz 1 says winners receive the price; the Final sheet says they pay it. The auction-side wording differs.
- [[Levelized Electricity Cost]]: Quiz 3 says capacity; the Final sheet says capacity factor. These are not interchangeable phrases.
- [[Energy Efficiency]]: Quiz 3 uses the heading Behavioral Failures; the Final sheet explicitly says the rebound effect is not a market failure.
- [[Electricity Pricing]]: The Final sheet refers to lower build cost within a Gas Price Drop section. The wording difference remains visible.

These are condensed revision notes. The wiki traces what the sources say rather than establishing universal theorems or filling missing context with invented facts.
