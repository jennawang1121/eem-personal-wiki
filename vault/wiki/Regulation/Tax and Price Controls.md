# Tax and Price Controls

The notes connect price controls and tax incidence to welfare and the distribution of costs.

- In their price-ceiling example, shortages and deadweight loss coexist with allocative inefficiency: goods need not reach buyers with the highest willingness to pay.
- The less elastic side bears more of a tax. The examples associate inelastic demand with buyers bearing more and inelastic supply with sellers bearing more.
- The sheets write **buyer share = supply slope / (supply slope + |demand slope|)**. Quiz 2 also tells the reader to use inverse-curve slopes. This is preserved as the source's formula; the slope convention and formula have not been independently reconciled here.
- Quiz 2 says a secondary market improves allocation, while the Final sheet says it fixes allocative cost. Both say shortage and deadweight loss remain in the example.
