# Market Power

Market power is defined as the ability to raise the price by restricting the quantity sold. The notes distinguish an individual price-taking firm's quantity reduction from a non-competitive firm's restriction of output: the former has no price effect in their model, while the latter can raise price.

*   In the short run, price volatility alone does not establish market power, as prices are determined by the intersection of supply and demand, and capacity constraints can cause prices to exceed marginal cost.
*   Monopoly profit maximization occurs where Marginal Revenue equals Marginal Cost ($MR=MC$), leading to a lower quantity produced and a higher price, resulting in deadweight loss compared to a competitive market.
*   For a linear demand function $P=a-bQ$, the marginal revenue is $MR=a-2bQ$, so the MR curve has twice the slope of the inverse demand curve.
*   Market power is strongest when both the market demand and the fringe supply are inelastic, as this implies the residual demand is most inelastic, granting the most pricing power.
