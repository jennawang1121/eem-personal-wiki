# Electricity Pricing

Electricity market ordering is determined by the marginal cost (MC) of the unit, with a merit order from low to high cost as Renewables $\rightarrow$ Nuclear $\rightarrow$ Coal $\rightarrow$ Gas. The market price is defined as the MC of the marginal unit dispatched, and producer surplus (PS) is calculated as Price minus MC. Fossil fuels are noted as typically setting the price.

*   Merit order for electricity markets is Renewables $\rightarrow$ Nuclear $\rightarrow$ Coal $\rightarrow$ Gas (low to high cost).
*   Market price equals the Marginal Cost (MC) of the most expensive unit dispatched.
*   Fossil fuels are typically responsible for setting the price.
*   In the short run, off-peak prices may fall (MC $\downarrow$) while peak prices remain unchanged due to capacity constraints.
*   In the long run, lower build costs and increased plant numbers can cause peak prices to fall more as capacity adjusts.
