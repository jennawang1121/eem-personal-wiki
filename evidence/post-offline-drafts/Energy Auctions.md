# Energy Auctions

Energy Auctions

Auctions involve different pricing mechanisms. In a Uniform Price Auction, all winners receive the clearing price, and competitive firms bid approximately equal to the marginal cost (MC). If demand decreases, the price will fall. In a Pay-As-Bid Auction, each winner pays their own bid, requiring firms to forecast the clearing price and bid above MC. If demand drops, the price may remain high because bids are already inflated. The Winner's Curse is relevant in common-value auctions, where an increased number of bidders raises the risk that someone will overestimate the value.

*   Uniform Price Auction: All winners receive the clearing price.
*   Competitive firms bid approximately equal to MC.
*   If demand drops unexpectedly, the price falls.
*   Pay-As-Bid Auction: Each winner pays their own bid.
*   Firms must forecast the clearing price and bid above MC.
*   If demand drops, the price stays high (bids are already inflated).
*   Winner's Curse occurs in common-value auctions; more bidders increase the risk of overestimation.
