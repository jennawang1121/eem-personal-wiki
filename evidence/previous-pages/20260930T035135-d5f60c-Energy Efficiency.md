# Energy Efficiency

The notes connect efficiency policy with incentives, information and behavior.

- The rebound effect means that greater efficiency can increase usage. Quiz 3 places it under “Behavioral Failures,” while the Final sheet explicitly says it is **not a market failure**. The heading alone should not override that distinction.
- The principal-agent example concerns a landlord who makes investment decisions while the tenant pays utility bills. The source does not specifically say “maintenance.”
- The CAFE standard applies to fleet-average fuel economy. The notes list technology improvements and sales mix as compliance routes.
- Credit trading allows over-compliance to be sold, as described in Quiz 3.
- The Final sheet distinguishes knowledge flowing to other firms from internal R&D sharing.
