# Tax and Price Controls

Price ceilings can cause shortages and deadweight loss (DWL), leading to allocative inefficiency. The welfare loss (allocative cost) occurs when goods are not allocated to buyers with the highest willingness to pay.

*   Tax Incidence: The less elastic side pays more of the tax.
*   Buyer Share Formula: Buyer share is calculated as Supply slope divided by (Supply slope plus the absolute value of the Demand slope).
*   Inelastic Demand: Buyers bear the tax (e.g., insulin, gasoline, electricity).
*   Inelastic Supply: Sellers bear the tax (e.g., land).
*   Price Ceiling Effects: Result in a shortage, deadweight loss, and allocative inefficiency.
*   Secondary Market: Can fix allocative cost but cannot eliminate the deadweight loss or the shortage.
