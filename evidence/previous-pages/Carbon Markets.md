# Carbon Markets

Carbon policy effects involve various mechanisms and behavioral considerations. Carbon taxes raise the marginal cost of fossil fuels, shifting supply up, and the fuel switching price usually increases depending on the marginal unit. Offsets increase compliance options, which can lead to a decrease in permit price and electricity price, thereby reducing the profit of low-cost generators.

*   Permit allocation methods affect market efficiency: Free permits can distort exit decisions, whereas auctioning is considered efficient.
*   Opportunity cost is the same regardless of allocation method: Free permits result in a cost equal to the market price.
*   In cap-and-trade equilibrium, the lower-cost firm (flatter Marginal Abatement Cost, MAC) abates more and buys fewer permits.
*   The total cost in cap-and-trade is calculated as $\Sigma(0.5 \times X_i \times P_i)$ for each firm.
*   Carbon leakage occurs when regulation causes production to move to a less-regulated region, while reshuffling involves trading partners changing without changing total production.
