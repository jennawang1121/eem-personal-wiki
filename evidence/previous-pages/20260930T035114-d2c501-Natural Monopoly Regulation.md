# Natural Monopoly Regulation

The notes describe a natural monopoly as a market whose demand lies within the declining-average-cost range, so one firm can supply it at the lowest cost.

- The notes compare MC and AC: AC falls when MC < AC and rises when MC > AC.
- In the stated cost model **C = F + cQ**, setting **P = MC = c** does not recover fixed cost: profit equals **−F**.
- Setting P = AC gives zero profit in the notes' example, with a welfare tradeoff relative to marginal-cost pricing.
- A two-part tariff separates the per-unit price from a fixed fee. The notes set the per-unit price at MC and use consumer surplus at that price to determine the maximum fixed fee. The fixed fee does not calculate consumer surplus.
- The Final sheet adds the condition **allowed return > cost of capital** when discussing capital overinvestment under rate-of-return regulation.
