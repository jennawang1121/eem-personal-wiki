# Futures and Hedging

Futures markets offer several advantages over forwards, including being exchange traded, standardized, and utilizing mark-to-market settlement, which reduces counterparty risk and provides high liquidity with low transaction costs. Forwards are described as over-the-counter private contracts, which involve higher counterparty risk and lower liquidity.

*   Futures contracts are traded on an exchange and are standardized.
*   Forwards are private contracts traded OTC and have higher counterparty risk.
*   Basis risk is the risk that a futures contract is poorly correlated with the actual asset price.
*   Hedging involves using futures contracts to offset exposure: short futures for selling a commodity and long futures for buying a commodity.
*   The hedge size is calculated as Exposure $\times$ Price Sensitivity to determine the required amount of currency.
