# Externalities and Policy

Externalities arise when private equilibrium (MB = MPC) ignores an external cost, leading to a social optimum where MB = MPC + MEC, resulting in a deadweight loss (DWL) calculated as $0.5 \times (Q_{\text{market}} - Q_{\text{social}}) \times \text{MEC}$. Solutions for market failures include pricing unpriced externalities with a Pigouvian tax, providing information to address imperfect information, and setting minimum efficiency standards to resolve principal-agent problems. Instrument choice depends on measurability: measurable emissions suggest a Tax or Cap-and-Trade, while non-measurable emissions suggest a Technology Mandate.
